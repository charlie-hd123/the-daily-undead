# Mystery Box art and motion

The box is a three-dimensional prop in the existing Daily Undead page. The real puzzle panel remains the application; no separate game UI or puzzle renderer was created. The chest has separate wooden boards, recessed joints, bevelled steel bands and trim, bolts, carrying handles, a clasp and a hinged lid. Three painted question marks remain on the front. No lettering is on the prop. The original approximately 105° opening angle is retained. The lid keeps its closed fit through 10% openness, then smoothly shortens by 20% along its hinge-to-edge axis by 65% openness, before its broad interior turns towards the player. Earlier compensation removes the mid-swing size bulge while retaining the approved fully open proportions. A projection regression checks every millisecond of the 850ms opening against the final height. This reduces the tall backboard effect while preserving the closed fit, width, thickness and camera. The shorter depth is held through the fading close and resets only with a closed pose.

The chest is the primary focal point. A warm key light keeps the wood readable, while cyan fill and violet side light catch its boards and metalwork. A softly lit floor in the actual 3D scene gives the stronger directional and contact shadows a visible surface. Its radial texture fades into the page; CSS ambient light extends the same cyan/violet atmosphere behind it. The matching loading still is exported from this scene, including its floor and lighting.

One compact invitation sits above the closed chest at every width. The title chooses one of the 10 approved phrases before first paint; “Your daily challenge awaits.” is the HTML fallback. It uses the site's Barlow Condensed at 600 weight, 22–28px (16px on short screens), a restrained ice-to-lilac gradient and balanced wrapping. Two title lines are reserved so every phrase keeps the composition stable. The title font is preloaded. A plain, muted “Tap box to open” hint sits directly beneath the title; neither line is a button or activates the chest. The group ends 18px above the projected lid (12px on short screens), and fades as opening begins. The hint hides immediately on tap, including an early queued tap, while retaining its line box until the invitation is removed. This keeps the translated group height and heading position fixed throughout the fade. Completion and fallback remove the whole group with the presentation. A shared horizontal alpha mask fades the scene's outermost 6% into the page, avoiding hard floor/shadow edges on narrow screens while leaving the entire prop opaque. The same mask applies to both the loading poster and live canvas.

The box is offered only for a fresh official daily puzzle. Just-for-fun puzzles go straight to play. A browser-local receipt is saved when opening begins, so refreshes and later visits to that day's official puzzle skip the box even before a guess has been made. Each new day can reveal its own official puzzle. Preview days use isolated preview storage; no score, puzzle or account progress is changed by the presentation. Unavailable persistence or a failed receipt write skips the presentation. Guarded storage keeps gameplay available in memory for that visit without overwriting older readable progress, with a notice explaining that the tab must stay open.

The player activates the chest to begin; it stays closed indefinitely until that gesture. The title fades as the lid opens; the prompt has already disappeared on tap. The caption uses the hidden attribute, so a retained touch-screen hover state cannot override its disappearance. A native button provides Enter/Space support and a keyboard focus ring, with a hit target fitted to the projected closed chest rather than the full canvas. An early click is remembered until the real puzzle, renderer, decoded icon and branded font are ready; repeated activation cannot restart the sequence. The actual **Quest Steps**, **Last Words** or **Blood Ties** icon rises through the white interior in the chest’s perspective, pauses for recognition, then arcs into the puzzle card’s top-right corner. It uses the same SVG artwork as the puzzle selection cards. Four thin CSS 3D image layers provide restrained depth. The three SVGs share a 184px painted width within their 240px viewBoxes, matching the unchanged Last Words reference. Quest Steps uses three X marks joined by a dotted treasure-map route; Blood Ties joins tiles 1–4–5 and 2–3–6 into two L shapes. During emergence all three use the unchanged Last Words 1.25 optical scale, so their visible widths match throughout the lift and hold. A shared base width cap of 168px or 62% of the chest opening and shared vertical/horizontal clearance accommodate all three silhouettes. Per-icon bounds include the original SVG strokes and exclude transparent margins; the artwork keeps its original proportions. Its orientation comes from the actual Three.js camera view direction, including the correct screen-Y and depth signs. The front face and extrusion keep that orientation throughout emergence, then flatten into the native corner position. A matrix regression test compares all three CSS axes against the actual Three.js view basis. As the chest starts its 550ms fade, the lid eases down from 105° to roughly 80°; it disappears before closing fully. The image has one constant grayscale/brightness treatment while emerging, travelling and sitting in the native panel; there is no blue recolour at landing. The thin sides and tilt resolve to zero as the icon shrinks and fades to the native watermark’s 12% opacity. The original image returns to its measured position without a duplicate handoff. The puzzle name appears normally with the panel. Its native corner icon has a 1.4rem right inset, with the heading reserving matching clearance. The ordinary puzzle surface, clue/letter board, scores and controls follow in a restrained stagger. While waiting, the real panel is absolutely positioned, clipped without creating an internal scroll container, and inert, so it remains measurable without reserving its full page height. The closed container is the scene height plus 16px; the statistics/footer sit directly below it. During the icon’s journey, the container eases to the measured native panel height over 500ms, completing before the panel starts appearing. The footer moves naturally in document flow. The final native panel has the same height and position when the temporary wrapper is removed. A stable desktop scrollbar gutter prevents width/target shifts as the page becomes scrollable. Completion, interruption and resizing restore the original content and accessibility attributes.

The original wide CSS crate had inconsistent perspective, flat surfaces and a large, bordered white light layer. The replacement uses one coherent camera, physically based materials, contact shadows and reflected light. The cool fill/rim lights relate it to the site's existing cyan/violet palette; white light originates inside the hollow chest. The final UI uses the existing font, surfaces, colours, clues and controls.

## Research and decisions

- [Nielsen Norman Group: The Role of Animation and Motion in UX](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-purpose-ux/) argues for brief, purposeful and unobtrusive motion. The sequence communicates a closed chest → player activation → opening → usable daily puzzle. The opening action is integrated with the chest; there is no modal, additional continue screen or looping effect.
- [NN/G: Duration and Motion Characteristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/) supports natural acceleration/deceleration and short interface transitions. The closed hold is now controlled by the player; once ready, activation begins the opening immediately without a further timer. The 850ms lid opening overlaps the beginning of the 1050ms icon lift, followed by a 340ms recognition pause, an 880ms journey to its corner position and a staggered appearance of the surrounding UI. The page height adjusts during the first 500ms of that journey, before the real panel fades in. The opening sequence finishes 3500ms after activation.
- [Three.js physically based material documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshStandardMaterial.html) explains roughness/metalness and image-based lighting. Those material differences, bevels and reflected light supply depth instead of painting glow over a flat rectangle.
- [The official Zombies overview](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/24121368/black-ops-6-zombies-what-you-need-to-know) describes the Mystery Box's random glowing reward. Here the daily puzzle replaces the weapon; puzzle selection and scoring are unchanged.
- [MDN WebGL best practices](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebGL_API/WebGL_best_practices) informed resolution limits, resource cleanup and avoiding a perpetual render loop.

## Assets and reproducibility

- `assets/mystery-box/aged-oak.webp`: original ImageGen material texture, converted to WebP. Built-in image generation was used, not the API/CLI fallback.
- `assets/mystery-box/chest-poster.webp`: transparent 1220×620 reference still rendered from the same 3D scene, camera and materials. The export uses a fixed 6.1/3.1 orthographic half-width/half-height, then restores the on-screen renderer. This appears in the initial HTML, before JavaScript or puzzle data is ready. Its fit matches the live scene on desktop and phone.
- `assets/mystery-box/painted-question.png`: fixed 256×256 transparent artwork, rasterized from the bundled Barlow Condensed 900 question-mark glyph with small deterministic wear flecks. Both the live prop and the exported poster use it. The renderer never draws system/web-font text, preventing cold-load font timing or platform font metrics from changing the marks. Regenerate with `python3 scripts/generate-mystery-box-paint.py` (Pillow required), then re-export the poster from closed review. The font's existing licence is in `assets/fonts/OFL.txt`.
- `js/mystery-box-scene.js`: prop, materials, camera and lighting.
- `js/mystery-box.js`: integration with the real panel, native activation and presentation lifecycle.
- `js/mystery-box-history.js`: browser-local, official-only daily presentation receipts, using the same isolated storage object as the application.
- `assets/dead-letters-card.svg`, `assets/quest-steps-card.svg` and `assets/blood-ties-card.svg`: shared selection, reward and puzzle-corner artwork. The actual image travels; a comment preserves its original DOM position.
- `js/mystery-box-motion.js`: icon trajectory, responsive fit, 3D pose and the screen-space front-rim portal. A shared paint envelope accounts for the SVGs’ transparent margins. Unit tests cover emergence, narrow/short stage fit, the recognition pause and exact final corner registration and opacity, compact-to-puzzle height timing, optical icon balance, full lid framing and poster/live camera registration. Activation tests cover waiting for a gesture, queuing an early tap, cancellation and one activation per puzzle.
- `scripts/vendor/mystery-box-three.mjs`: explicit Three.js exports. Rebuild the checked-in browser bundle with `pnpm run build:mystery-box-vendor`. Three.js is locked in `pnpm-lock.yaml`; its MIT licence accompanies the bundle.

## Responsive behaviour and fallback

The invitation follows the visible closed-lid top in the same camera projection, with the same centred hierarchy on phones, tablets and desktops. CSS percentage margins use the component's inline size, matching desktop and phone fits before the renderer loads. The reserved two-line title and non-interactive hint fit above the prop at 320px minimum width, including short screens. Phone stages are 250px high (200px on short screens), keeping the portrait composition compact. The closed component adds only 16px below the stage. The existing minimum site width is 320px; very short screens can scroll vertically. The orthographic fit uses a minimum half-height of 3.1 world units. The conservative unshortened prop sweep was measured through 121 hinge positions: the top peaks near 97°, before the original unshortened final 105° pose, at camera Y 2.828978. The new fit leaves at least 8px above that peak at the 200px minimum stage height, including wide landscape screens. Automated tests cover the measured swept envelope, responsive camera fits and poster/live registration. Local review attributes expose the projected real prop bounds; the fading light beam is excluded. The orthographic fit protects the fully open lid as well as the closed case. Phone framing is larger relative to the available width. Short viewports fit the prop to both dimensions. Pixel ratio is capped (1.5 on phones, 1.75 elsewhere), shadow resolution is limited, and frames render only during the brief opening and when the stage resizes. There is no always-running animation loop.

The loading still and first live frame exchange visibility atomically. They are not cross-faded: fading two transparent layers at once briefly exposes the page background through the otherwise solid chest. The live scene is drawn in the same frame that makes its canvas visible. An unchanged stage size does not reset the WebGL backing buffer when the stage moves into the real puzzle panel.

A synchronous entry check runs before the body paints. Opened/started/completed puzzles, remembered accounts awaiting cloud progress, reduced motion, missing animation capabilities and unavailable persistence use a quiet native loading message. Only a confirmed fresh official puzzle imports Three.js or initializes the renderer and textures. The font is ready before measuring the native panel and the actual SVG is decoded before opening, preventing late asset swaps during flight. If an asset is unavailable, if initialization exceeds its 1800ms deadline, or if the graphics context is lost, the presentation is cancelled and the ordinary puzzle appears directly, with no poster animation, required click or panel transition. A renderer that arrives after the deadline cannot restart the presentation. The matching still is only a loading placeholder while 3D initializes. Reduced motion also goes directly to the real puzzle after data loading. Hidden puzzle controls and the travelling icon are inert; letter shortcuts ignore that period, preventing invisible guesses. The panel becomes interactive once the reward and controls have settled. Resize during flight returns the icon immediately to normal play. Eligible official presentations start at the top of the page; just-for-fun entry never runs a presentation. Focus is preserved, and resources are cleaned up on page exit.

Local QA can freeze the real component with `?boxReview=closed` or `?boxReview=open`. Adding `&boxFrame=0` through `&boxFrame=850` to open review freezes the exact opening millisecond using the real pose functions; projected lid height, depth scale and openness are exposed alongside the existing local prop bounds. These frame controls are ignored outside local review. Closed review has a Render poster control to preview/export the current canvas image for replacing the still after geometry changes. A Show loading image / Show live 3D control compares the actual poster and closed canvas in the same layout, so their registration can be checked on different viewport shapes. `?boxReview=fallback` exercises the direct-to-puzzle path without loading the 3D renderer. These parameters are ignored outside localhost/127.0.0.1. They do not simulate puzzle data or account writes. Screenshots from frozen poses should be described as visual review, not as normal-play timing tests.

`?boxReview=lift`, `?boxReview=reward` and `?boxReview=landed` freeze the real icon partway out, above the chest or in its final corner position. These use the actual application icon and puzzle panel. A resize cancels frozen flight just as it does normal flight; reload at the new viewport to review that size.

## Returning-player entry

`js/player-entry.js` supplies the pre-paint entry decision and guarded browser storage. The loading poster is hidden by default and is allowed only after that decision. A fresh eligible entry acquires a real WebGL 2 context before enabling the poster; an advertised API with a blocked, throwing or already-lost graphics context stays on quiet loading and proceeds directly to the question. Three.js reuses that same canvas/context, rather than probing and allocating a second one. Returning players never allocate a context. Remembered accounts stay quiet until cloud state is resolved; a newly confirmed fresh puzzle must pass the context check before any box wrapper is inserted. Later asset/network failures or a graphics context lost after presentation has started still cancel the presentation safely.

`js/browser-storage.js` shares the same storage facade with gameplay, account/session helpers, Supabase authentication and community identities. Successful writes are verified; failed writes stay in memory, while successful reads are not cached across tabs. Anonymous community identities are not recreated from ephemeral memory. The box receipt remains cosmetic and browser-local; ordinary cloud puzzle progress still suppresses an already-started puzzle on another device. A storage event releases another waiting tab directly into its question.

`js/play-destination.js` remembers the active official puzzle, just-for-fun puzzle or completed hub by date. A reload restores that destination after ordinary state/account loading, validating the official completion gate before resuming just-for-fun. It never changes official assignments, unlocks an unfinished day, or changes scores. Destinations and receipts use the existing preview-scoped storage for developer dates.
